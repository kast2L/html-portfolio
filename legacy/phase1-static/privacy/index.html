This page explains what william.llcdouglashill.com collects, where it goes, and how long it is kept. It covers the whole site.

Who runs this site

Douglas Hill Consulting LLC, based in Tempe, Arizona, runs this site. In this policy, “I” and “me” mean Douglas Hill, acting for the business.

Send privacy questions to DouglasHill@llcdouglashill.com.

What the sprint request form collects

The request form on the Process Automation Sprint page asks for:

  • your name
  • your work email address
  • your company
  • a description of the process you want fixed.

When you send it, the page adds:

  • the campaign tags in the link you followed, if it had any (utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign, utm_term). I add these tags to the links I send so I can tell which outreach brought you here;
  • a Google Ads click ID (gclid), if the link you followed had one;
  • the path of the page you sent the form from.

Your IP address, browser details, and cookies are not stored with your request. The form also has a hidden field that should stay empty, and it reports how long the page was open before you sent it. Both are spam checks. Neither is stored.

Why I use it

I use your request to reply to you, to judge whether the sprint fits and prepare for a call, and to see which outreach channels bring in requests. I do not sell it, add you to a mailing list, or use it for advertising.

Where form data goes

Your request travels from your browser through Amazon CloudFront and Amazon API Gateway to an AWS Lambda function in my own Amazon Web Services (AWS) account. From there:

  • It is stored in Amazon DynamoDB in the AWS region US East (N. Virginia), us-east-1.
  • A copy is emailed to me through Amazon SES. That email lands in my Microsoft 365 mailbox, which Microsoft holds as my email provider. The site does not send any email to you.
  • Amazon CloudWatch keeps operational logs for 30 days. Each log line records a request ID, whether the request worked, and the length of each field. The logs never contain what you typed.
  • API Gateway access logging is off, so the form’s API does not log IP addresses.
  • I copy a short summary of your request into the tracker I use to follow up on outreach.

AWS handles this data as my service provider. The AWS Privacy Notice describes how AWS treats it.

The contact page form

The form on the contact page is a separate form. Formspree handles it and stores those messages under its own retention rules and its privacy policy.

On the contact page, the booking button opens Calendly in a window over the page. Doing so loads Calendly’s code from Calendly’s servers.

Analytics

Google Analytics 4 runs on four pages: the Process Automation Sprint page, the page you see after sending the request form, this page, and the terms of use. The other pages on the site do not load it. On those four pages it records:

  • which pages you viewed, and the page that sent you here
  • two actions: clicking a booking link, and sending a request form that the server confirmed it received
  • your device type, browser type, and approximate location
  • the campaign tags described above.

Nothing you type into the form is sent to Google Analytics.

Google Analytics sets cookies on this site named _ga and _ga_ followed by an ID. Google signals and advertising features are turned off, and the analytics property is not linked to Google Ads.

If your browser sends the Global Privacy Control signal, these pages do not load Google Analytics at all. You can also opt out with Google’s opt-out browser add-on, or by blocking cookies or scripts in your browser. The Google Privacy Policy covers how Google treats this data.

Storage in your browser

On the four pages listed under analytics, a script saves the campaign tags and ad click ID from the link you followed, and the time it saved them, under one key in your browser’s local storage. It uses them to fill the form’s hidden fields and to add the campaign tags (not the ad click ID) to booking links. It never stores anything you type into the form.

After 30 days, the script deletes the saved values the next time you open one of those pages. You can delete them sooner by clearing this site’s data in your browser settings.

Booking a call

Booking links open Calendly, a separate scheduling service, in a new tab. Calendly collects what you enter when you book, such as your name, email address, and answers to its questions, and shares it with me. The booking link carries the campaign tags described above. No Calendly code runs on the four pages listed under analytics. Calendly’s privacy notice covers your booking.

Fonts

Pages on this site load their fonts from Google Fonts. Your browser fetches the fonts from Google’s servers, which receive your IP address and browser details with that request. Google explains this in its Google Fonts privacy notes.
