Questions before you book

Most of your experience is government and Fortune 50. Is this sized for a company like ours?

Yes. The pain is the same: a process that runs on email and a shared spreadsheet. The sprint is sized for one process and one team. What carries over from larger organizations is the habit of building it governed and documented, without their price or timeline.

You work alone. What happens if something goes wrong or you're unavailable?

Everything is built in your tenant from the first day and documented as it goes, so nothing is held on my systems. You pay by milestone. If I don't deliver the design, your booking payment is refunded in full. If I'm unavailable for an extended stretch, you can end the engagement and keep the work built so far. There is no backup consultant, and you should know that before you book.

You deliver part-time. Will our project slip?

The 4-week calendar is fixed before we start, and only one sprint is in delivery at a time. All the work lives in your tenant, and you never pay ahead of delivered work.

Do we need new licenses?

Not for the standard sprint. It uses SharePoint and standard Microsoft 365 connectors, which are covered by Microsoft 365 licenses that include Power Automate. Approvers don't need a premium license. If your process needs Dataverse or a premium connector, that is outside this offer, and the call tells you so before anything is signed.

What does it cost, and how do we pay?

One fixed price for the whole sprint, confirmed in writing before you sign. You pay 30% to book your start date, 40% when the core path works in a live demo, and 30% when you accept go-live. A milestone counts as accepted if you neither sign off nor report a failing checklist item within 5 business days. If you don't want to go ahead after seeing the design, you stop there and keep it.

What if our process isn't a good fit?

The scoping call is there to find that out. If the sprint isn't the right fit, or the process isn't worth automating, I'll tell you. No pitch, no obligation.

Couldn't we build this ourselves in Power Automate?

Sometimes, yes. The sprint is for the process that has resisted that, or one that has to be built right and documented within weeks. The call gives you an honest answer either way.

Our IT team doesn't let business teams build things. Where does this live, and who owns it?

It is built in your existing Microsoft 365 environment, in an environment your admin chooses, and designed with IT. The flows are owned by an account you choose, not mine. The governance habits come from work across 200+ environments at a Fortune 50 healthcare company.

What if we need more than the sprint covers?

Anything beyond the agreed scope is written down as a change request, with the effort in hours and the effect on dates. You choose to swap it for something of similar size, park it for later, or add it. Nothing extra is built or billed without your written approval.

What happens after go-live?

For 30 days, defects against the signed checklist are fixed at no charge. The runbook shows your team how to change approvers and thresholds themselves. After that, more help is available by request.
